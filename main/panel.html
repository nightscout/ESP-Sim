The encrypted firmware (V05, V35) talks only under a session key that the phone and pump agree in the pump's key exchange, and the pump runs that exchange only for a server key it trusts. This simulated pump trusts its own, made at random on this board (no real pump's key); the panel does not hand it out, so a phone cannot run the key exchange with it yet. The clear-text firmware (V65, V03, V02) needs none of this. The pump pairs over Bluetooth by passkey: type the number shown at the top of this card.

New pod

Clears pairing and delivery: the next phone pairs it as a fresh pod. Deactivating the pod from the app does the same.

Made-up readings. Use only a test phone that controls no real pump or pod: an app reading this sensor would dose insulin from these numbers.

A reading every 5 minutes. The virtual person eats on their own (breakfast, lunch, dinner, snacks, from a list of foods), treats their own lows, sometimes exercises; their glucose follows the insulin they get. "From the simulated pump" means only what the pod or YpsoPump delivers: the AID's decisions show in the glucose (the pod counts its boluses and its running basal; its temp basals are not modelled yet). A toy model for testing software, not a physiological simulator.
G6: enter the transmitter ID in the app. G7: the app finds the sensor (DXCM…) by itself. Both are found alongside the pod.

Wi-Fi

Joining a network gives the pod the right time and puts this panel on your LAN; the board prints its address on the serial console. Without one it runs its own access point.
